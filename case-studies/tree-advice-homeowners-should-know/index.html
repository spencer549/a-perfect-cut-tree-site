The Advice Nobody Told You About Trees, But A Perfect Cut Tree Service Will

July 30, 2026

Most people call an experienced tree company in Louisiana when something is already wrong: a limb over the roof, a trunk showing rot, a root system buckling the walkway. The call happens because the problem has already become visible, sometimes urgent, occasionally dangerous.

The following 4 case studies document real situations faced by some of our real clients. Each one reveals a different dimension of what professional tree service actually looks like when the company doing the work genuinely cares about the outcome.

What Full-Service Tree Care Actually Requires

The scope of a professional tree job extends well beyond the moment the saw runs. From the first site visit to the final sweep of the property, a complete engagement includes:

Assessment and advisory:

  • Species identification and root system evaluation
  • Structural risk analysis for nearby foundations, fencing, and buildings
  • Transparent client communication about short- and long-term implications
  • Honest guidance even when it contradicts what a client was hoping to hear

Execution:

  • Equipment selection matched to site constraints, not just what’s available on the truck
  • Crew coordination to manage debris, direction of fall, and access limitations
  • Careful operation in tight spaces, around buildings, fences, pools, and other structures
  • Stump grinding to remove regrowth risk and eliminate below-grade obstruction

Completion:

  • Full debris removal limbs, leaves, grindings, and all material generated on-site
  • Property walkthrough to confirm no incidental damage occurred
  • Direct confirmation from ownership or management that the client is satisfied
  • Site left in a condition the homeowner is genuinely pleased with

Each case below reflects how this standard holds across very different types of jobs.

Case Study 01: The Tree That Would Have Cost a Family Their Home

The Situation

The client, Sid B. and his wife were navigating something most people only do once: buying their first home. They had found a property that met everything on their list. A pre-purchase inspection flagged some foundation concerns and close to the house stood a 40-foot Cypress tree.

Cypress root systems are aggressive and wide-reaching. When planted in proximity to a structure, they do not simply grow beside the foundation they grow beneath it, and they continue doing so long after the visible portion of the tree is gone. What looks like a resolved problem on the surface can be an accelerating one underground. Sid had done his research. He read reviews, weighed his options, and called A Perfect Cut Tree Service.

What Happened Next

Roger, the owner, visited the property and gave Sid and his wife a thorough assessment. He confirmed that the tree could be removed. He also told them something most contractors in his position would not have said: that removing the Cypress would not resolve the underlying issue.

The root mass already embedded beneath the slab would continue to break down over the following decade or more. As it deteriorated, it would create void/empty space where soil and organic matter had once been and the foundation sitting above those voids would eventually settle into them. The house would sink. Removing the tree would eliminate the visible concern on the inspection report. It would not prevent the structural consequence. Roger made sure Sid understood this clearly before any decision was made.

The Outcome

  • Sid and his wife did not purchase the property. The information Roger provided changed the financial picture entirely and protected them from what Sid described as a potentially devastating mistake.No work was performed. No invoice was generated.
  • Roger offered knowledge and honesty to a young couple he had never met, on a job that would never become a job.
  • Sid’s review captures exactly what that meant to him: a professional who cared more about the outcome than the transaction. He has since spoken about A Perfect Cut Tree Service as his unconditional recommendation to anyone with a tree-related concern.

Why This Matters

This case does not fit the conventional definition of a completed tree job because no tree was cut. What it illustrates is something more fundamental: the standard of integrity that defines how A Perfect Cut Tree Service operates. A company willing to lose a job to protect a client’s interests is a company worth trusting with the jobs it does take on.

Case Study 02: Palm Tree Removal Behind a Freestanding Building in a Confined Yard

The Situation

The client, Larry L. needed work done in the most inconvenient corner of his property.

  • One palm tree required full removal
  • Four additional palms needed trimming

All of them were located in the far rear corner of his lot, positioned behind a freestanding shop building that stood between the trees and any straightforward access route. Situations like this expose the difference between a crew that adapts and a crew that either damages things trying or simply tells the client the job is not possible. The variables compound quickly:

  • clearance for equipment
  • proximity to the building
  • the angle and direction of any falling material
  • the condition of the ground between the access point and the work zone

Execution

A Perfect Cut Tree Service arrived with machinery and equipment selected specifically for the constraints of the site. Rather than forcing standard tools into a non-standard space, our crew came prepared for the access limitations they knew they would encounter.

Throughout the job, the crew worked with the kind of deliberateness Larry noted, specifically meticulous, clean, and careful about the property around them, not just the trees in front of them. No damage was done to the building, the fencing, the yard, or any of the surrounding plantings. Everything generated by the job was cleared before the crew left.

Results

  • One palm tree fully removed from a difficult rear-corner access location
  • 4 palm trees trimmed cleanly and without damage to surrounding structures
  • Right equipment deployed from the outset on improvised solutions
  • Property left spotless after completion
  • Client stated he would highly recommend the company and described their capability as complete: the tools and the talent to handle any tree service need.

Case Study 03: Two Citrus Trees Removed and Gone in Under 25 Minutes

The Situation

The client, Alison A. needed two citrus trees removed from her property. The trees had been ailing, no longer productive, structurally compromised, and overdue for removal. She reached out to A Perfect Cut Tree Service on a Thursday. By Friday, she had a proposal. The pricing she described as quite reasonable.

Execution

Our crew member Roger followed up the day after sending the proposal to confirm scheduling. On the day of the job, the crew led by our crew member- Chad texted Alison directly to let her know the team was en route, a small gesture that eliminates the window of uncertainty most homeowners dread about contractor arrival times. Our crew arrived and went to work. Both citrus trees were cut down and removed.

Efficiency of this kind does not happen by accident. It is the product of a crew that arrives knowing exactly what needs to happen, with equipment already prepared for the job, in a sequence that has been thought through before the first cut is made.

Chad also informed Alison that the team would return later in the week to complete stump removal and grinding, keeping her informed of the full timeline rather than leaving her to wonder what came next.

Results

  • Two ailing citrus trees removed in less than 25 minutes from crew arrival
  • Clear communication from proposal through scheduling to day-of arrival notification
  • Stump grinding scheduled and communicated proactively as a follow-on step
  • Client described the overall experience as smooth, swift, and genuinely impressive
  • Alison specifically called out Roger, Chad, and the full team by name in her review

Case Study 04: Three Large Trees, Full Stump Grinding, and an Owner Who Showed Up Twice

The Situation

Maria I. hired A Perfect Cut Tree Service to remove three large trees from her property. The scale of the job meant significant volume, large trunk sections, extensive limb material, a substantial amount of ground debris and the kind of cleanup burden that many homeowners underestimate before the work begins.

She had a specific concern as well, though perhaps not an unusual one:

  • Would the company she hired actually follow through on every element of the job?
  • Would certain things get left behind as something for her to deal with later?

Execution

  • The crew handled every component of the removal. Trees came down. Stumps were ground. Limbs, leaves, and all remaining debris were loaded and hauled away. Maria described it plainly: she did not have to worry about a thing.
  • What stood out most in her account was not the scope of the work, it was the owner’s presence. The owner arrived at the beginning of the job to get the crew set up and the work underway. He came back at the end, after completion, to walk the property personally and confirm that Maria’s expectations had been met.
  • This is not standard practice in the industry. Most jobs, even well-executed ones, conclude when the last truck leaves. The owner’s decision to return and check in directly reflects a level of accountability that clients experience as meaningful, even when everything went perfectly.

Results

  • Three large trees fully removed from the property
  • Complete stump grinding included as part of the scope
  • All limbs, leaves, and debris hauled away with nothing left on-site
  • Owner present at job start and personally returned at job end to confirm client satisfaction

What Keeps Clients Coming Back and Sending Their Neighbors to A Perfect Cut Tree Service

Across four completely different situations, one consistent picture emerges of how A Perfect Cut Tree Service approaches its work.

  • Honesty Over Revenue: The Cypress case is the clearest expression of this, but it shows up across the board. Clients are told what they need to know, not what is convenient to say. Alison got a proposal the same day she called. Larry got an honest assessment of what the job required in terms of equipment and access. Maria got a direct conversation at the close of the job rather than an assumption that everything was fine.
  • Preparation That Matches the Job: Larry’s corner access situation required the right machinery. Maria’s three-tree removal required full debris management. Alison’s job required a crew that knew how to move fast without being careless. None of these outcomes happen if the team is guessing or improvising. They happen because the work was thought through before the crew arrived.
  • Communication that Runs the Full Length of the Engagement: Chad texting Alison on the morning of her job. Roger called the day after sending a proposal. The owner returns at the close of Maria’s job. These are not accidental touches; they are a consistent pattern of keeping clients informed and ensuring nothing falls through the gaps.
  • Site completion as a non-negotiable standard: In every case, the property was left in a condition the homeowner was genuinely satisfied with. Not mostly cleared, not cleared enough fully completed, with nothing left behind.
