Safety first

What to Photograph From a Safe Distance

Don’t go near unstable trees or downed power lines to take photos. If a line is down or touching the tree, call your electric utility before anything else.

  1. Visible hazards

    Cracks, splits, hanging limbs, and anything over the house or driveway.

  2. Leaning

    A new lean, lifting soil or exposed roots at the base.

  3. Dead limbs

    Bare, brittle or broken branches in the canopy.

  4. Storm damage

    Fallen trees, torn limbs, and debris after severe weather.

  5. Disease symptoms

    Thinning leaves, fungus, bark damage or decay.

  6. Access

    How our crew and equipment can reach the tree.

  7. What’s nearby

    Roofs, fences, driveways, and power lines close to the tree.

Storm Tree Service Questions

Immediate hazard? Call us. For everything else, send photos of what you can see.

Is it safe to take photos of storm damage?

Only take photos from a safe distance. Don’t walk up to unstable trees, climb, move branches, or film near downed power lines.

What if the damage is urgent?

Call (504) 732-0779 right away. For an immediate hazard, don’t wait on a photo review.

What if a tree is touching a power line?

Stay well back, treat the line as live, and report it to your power company. Then call us. Don’t try to photograph it up close.

Can you tell from a video whether the tree must come down?

Photos and video give us a first look. We may need to see the tree in person before a final recommendation, and the safest option could be stabilizing, pruning, cleanup, or removal.

Are my photos private?

We use your files to review your tree-care request.

What photos and videos should I send?

Photos and short clips straight from your phone are fine (JPG, PNG, HEIC, MP4, or MOV). One shot of the whole tree, one close-up of the problem, and one of what’s nearby usually tell us the most.

Does a photo review replace an onsite inspection?

No. Photos give us a first look. Some trees need an in-person visit before we can make a final recommendation.

What happens after I upload?

Our team reviews what you sent and gets back to you with the next step: a follow-up question, an in-person assessment, or an estimate.

Does sending photos commit me to anything?

No. Sending photos doesn’t commit you to any work.
