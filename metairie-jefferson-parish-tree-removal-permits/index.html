Tree Removal Permits in Metairie and Jefferson Parish

June 12, 2026Local RegulationsTree Care TipsTree Removal

What homeowners need to know before scheduling any work

Tree removal in Metairie and Jefferson Parish is not always as simple as calling a contractor and getting it done. The mature live oaks, magnolias, and bald cypresses you see across the parish are part of what makes this area look and feel the way it does, and local rules reflect that. Some removals are completely routine. Others need a permit, an arborist report, replacement planting, or all three.

Key Takeaways

  • Tree removal rules in Jefferson Parish can apply based on a tree’s size, species, or location, even if it’s on your private property.
  • Live oaks are protected in many Louisiana communities because of their lifespan, ecological value, and cultural importance. Requirements vary, so always verify locally.
  • Trees in public rights-of-way, drainage easements, utility easements, or wetland zones are subject to extra review regardless of the rest of your property.
  • Removing a regulated tree without a permit can mean fines, required replacement planting, and problems when you go to sell the property.
  • The most important thing you can do before removing any mature tree is call the parish and confirm what applies to your specific tree and location.

Why These Rules Exist

Tree canopy in the Greater New Orleans area does real, measurable work:

  • Storm protection: Mature trees reduce wind speed at ground level and help buffer structures during tropical storms.
  • Flood management: Trees absorb rainfall and slow runoff in an area that already deals with significant flooding.
  • Heat relief: The urban canopy brings surface temperatures down during brutal Louisiana summers.
  • Property value: Mature trees consistently show up in research as a driver of neighborhood desirability.
  • Cultural identity: Live oaks have defined this region for centuries. Some specimens are older than any structure around them.

Local ordinances try to protect those benefits without making routine property maintenance impossible. The result is a system that treats each removal differently depending on the tree, where it sits, and why it needs to come down.

When You Typically Need a Permit

Requirements change over time, so confirm everything with the parish before you schedule anything. That said, these are the situations that most commonly require extra steps.

Trees Over a Certain Size

Most ordinances set a size threshold based on trunk diameter at breast height, or DBH, measured 4.5 feet above the ground. Trees below that threshold are usually unregulated. Trees above it may need an inspection, a permit application, or replacement planting before any work starts.

Protected Species

Some trees are protected regardless of size. Live oak is the most common example in Louisiana, but bald cypress and southern magnolia may also be protected depending on the jurisdiction. Historically significant trees can be protected on a case-by-case basis too.

Right-of-Way Trees

The strip of grass between the sidewalk and the street is usually parish property, not yours. Trees growing there are not yours to remove, even if they are causing problems on your side. The parish controls those removals.

Drainage and Utility Easements

If a tree sits inside a drainage or utility easement, you will likely need to coordinate with the parish, the utility company, or both before anything gets cut.

Wetland Zones

Parts of Jefferson Parish fall under state or federal wetland regulations. Removals in those areas can require environmental review on top of everything else.

Dead, Diseased, or Hazardous Trees

Most ordinances make room for emergency removals when a tree poses an immediate danger. You will still typically need documentation from a certified arborist, but the review process is usually faster than a standard permit application.

How to Find Out What Applies to Your Property

The only reliable way is to go straight to the source. Here is who to contact:

  • Jefferson Parish Department of Building Permits: Confirm whether your tree is regulated, what permit is required, and what to submit
  • Jefferson Parish Code Compliance and Enforcement: For questions about local ordinance compliance and regulated tree removals
  • Your local utility: If the tree is near power lines, service drops, or within a utility easement
  • Louisiana Department of Agriculture and Forestry: For state-level questions or assistance from the Office of Forestry
  • A certified arborist: For a written condition assessment that supports your permit application

If your property is in an incorporated city within the parish, including Kenner, Gretna, Westwego, or Harahan, check with that city directly. Municipal rules may differ from parish rules.

Why Skipping the Permit Is a Bad Idea

The penalties for removing a regulated tree without authorization can easily exceed what a permitted removal would have cost:

  • Fines: Local ordinances can impose significant penalties. The amount varies by jurisdiction.
  • Replacement planting: Often calculated by the diameter of the removed tree, which can mean multiple large replacement trees at your expense.
  • Property sale complications: Unresolved permit issues can show up during title searches and stall or kill a sale.
  • Civil liability: Removing a right-of-way tree, a neighbor’s tree, or an easement tree without authorization can expose you to legal claims.
  • Insurance issues: Some homeowner policies require permit compliance to cover contractor-caused damage.

A reputable contractor will not touch regulated work without seeing the permit first. If a contractor is pushing you to skip that step, that alone tells you something.

What Good Contractors Do

A professional Metairie tree service will, before any regulated removal:

  • Ask whether you have confirmed permit requirements with the parish
  • Provide a written estimate that includes the tree’s size, species, and reason for removal
  • Offer documentation suitable for a permit application if needed
  • Provide insurance certificates directly from their carrier
  • Confirm any required utility coordination

Be especially cautious about door-to-door contractors who show up after a storm and pressure you to act fast. That is the highest-risk scenario for skipping steps that matter.

The Most Common Mistakes We See

The most frequent issue is a homeowner who hires the cheapest contractor without checking permit requirements first, then discovers after the fact that the live oak needed a permit and replacement planting. The fine plus replacement cost usually runs higher than a permitted removal would have.

The second most common issue is confusion about right-of-way trees. A tree in the grass strip between your sidewalk and the street may look like it is on your property. It usually is not. Removing it without authorization can come with serious penalties.

The smoothest removals are the ones where the homeowner called the parish first, got a clear answer, and worked with a certified arborist who handled the documentation.

What to Do Right Now

  • Identify any mature live oak or other potentially regulated tree on your property
  • Note the trunk diameter at 4.5 feet above ground (DBH) for each one
  • Note the location relative to the right-of-way, easements, and property line
  • Contact the parish or your municipality to confirm whether removal requires a permit
  • Get written estimates only from licensed, insured contractors, and only after you know the permit requirements
  • Take dated photos of any tree you may want to remove in the future

What not to do

  • Do not authorize removal of any mature tree without confirming local requirements first
  • Do not assume a tree on your property is yours to remove
  • Do not hire a door-to-door contractor right after a storm without verifying their credentials
  • Do not take a contractor’s word for whether a permit is needed

When to Call a Certified Arborist

A certified arborist can provide the written assessment most permit applications require, document tree condition and hazard risk, and flag regulatory issues before you schedule any work. You can verify credentials through the ISA Find an Arborist directory.

You especially want an arborist involved when:

  • A mature live oak or other protected species is involved
  • The tree is dead, declining, or structurally compromised
  • The removal is near a regulated easement or right-of-way
  • The property is being prepared for construction or sale
  • The removal is tied to a hurricane damage or insurance claim

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a permit to remove a tree in Metairie?

Maybe. It depends on the tree’s size, species, location, and condition. Trees in rights-of-way, utility easements, or drainage easements are subject to additional review. Confirm current requirements directly with Jefferson Parish before scheduling any work.

Are live oaks protected in Jefferson Parish?

Live oaks are commonly subject to additional protection under local tree ordinances because of their size, longevity, and importance to the regional landscape. The specifics depend on your location and the circumstances of the removal. Always verify with the parish first.

Who approves tree removal permits in Metairie?

Permit review is typically handled by the Jefferson Parish Department of Building Permits. Depending on the tree’s location, public works, utilities, or other agencies may also need to weigh in. Contact the parish directly to confirm the current process.

Can an arborist help with a permit application?

Yes. A certified arborist can assess the tree, document its condition, write up a formal report, and identify issues that affect the permit process. This is especially useful for trees that are dead, declining, storm-damaged, or otherwise flagged for additional review.

Does homeowners insurance cover permit fees?

Permit fees are generally not covered under standard homeowners policies. Coverage for tree removal varies by policy and situation, particularly when a tree has damaged a structure. Check directly with your insurance provider.

Can I remove a tree in the right-of-way in front of my house?

In most cases, no. Trees in a public right-of-way are parish property, and removal requires authorization from the parish or the responsible municipal authority. Confirm ownership and applicable rules before scheduling anything.

What if my neighbor’s tree is damaging my property?

Disputes over overhanging branches, root encroachment, or property damage can involve legal considerations that vary by situation. Talk to a qualified attorney if you need guidance on a specific dispute.
