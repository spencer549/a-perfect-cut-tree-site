Benefits of Regular Tree and Shrub Pruning

  • Promotes Healthy Growth: Pruning helps trees and shrubs grow stronger by removing dead or diseased branches and any crossing or rubbing limbs that cause damage or hinder growth.
  • Enhances Appearance: Pruning shapes trees and shrubs for a cleaner, more manicured look and better curb appeal.
  • Increases Sunlight and Airflow: Opening up a tree's canopy lets more sunlight reach the ground and the lower branches. Better airflow also helps prevent fungal diseases.
  • Reduces Hazards: Dead or damaged branches are a safety hazard, especially during storms. Regular pruning finds and removes them, lowering the risk of property damage or injury.
  • Saves Money in the Long Run: Fixing small problems early prevents larger, more costly ones. Regular pruning can keep trees and shrubs healthy and head off more extensive (and expensive) work later.

Different Types of Tree and Shrub Pruning

Each type of pruning has its own purpose.

Crown Thinning

Crown thinning selectively removes branches within a tree's canopy to let in more light and air. It also lightens the crown and helps prevent damage from strong winds.

Crown Raising

Crown raising removes lower branches to increase clearance for pedestrians, vehicles, or buildings. It can also open up sightlines and views.

Crown Reduction

Crown reduction shrinks the overall size of a tree's canopy by selectively removing branches. It controls the tree's size, improves its shape, and lowers the risk of branch failure.

Shrub Shearing

Shearing uses electric or gas-powered hedge trimmers to give a shrub a formal, manicured shape. It's common for hedges and formal gardens.

Shrub Renewal

This involves removing older, woody growth from a shrub to promote new, healthier growth. Shrub renewal can help keep shrubs from becoming too leggy or overgrown, and can improve their overall health and appearance.

Deadwooding

Deadwooding removes dead or dying branches from a tree or shrub. It prevents hazards and improves the plant's health and appearance.

Tools and Equipment We Use for Pruning

Here are the pruning tools and equipment we use to work safely and cleanly:

  • Pruning Shears: Also called hand pruners, these handle smaller branches and shrub shaping. We keep the blades sharp for precise cuts that do little damage to the plant.
  • Loppers: Loppers have longer handles and larger blades than pruning shears, so they can cut larger branches. We use loppers with extendable handles and non-slip grips to reach high branches safely.
  • Pruning Saws: Pruning saws cut thicker branches and stems. We use professional-grade saws with sharp, curved blades for clean cuts.
  • Pole Pruners: Pole pruners reach high branches without a ladder. Ours have adjustable lengths and sharp blades.
  • Safety Equipment: Pruning can be dangerous, so we always use personal protective equipment (PPE), including hard hats, eye and ear protection, and gloves.

For larger pruning jobs, we also use aerial lifts and chippers.
